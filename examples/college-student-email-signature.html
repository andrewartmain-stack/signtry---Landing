College student email signature examples

A good college student email signature tells professors, recruiters and internship managers exactly who you are: your name, your major, your university and your graduation year. Below are 10 examples for undergraduates, graduate students and PhD candidates that you can copy.

Updated October 28, 2026 · 6 min read · By the SignTry team

Quick answer: A college student email signature should include your full name, degree and major, university, expected graduation year, and one way to reach you, plus a LinkedIn or portfolio link if you are job hunting. Keep it to four or five lines.

Use your university email address when you write to professors and campus offices. It looks more professional and is less likely to land in spam.

What to leave out of a student email signature

  • Quotes and song lyrics. Save them for your personal email.
  • Every club you have joined. Pick one role that matters for the person you are writing to.
  • Large images. They load slowly and often arrive as attachments.
  • Your GPA, unless you are applying for jobs and it is strong.
  • Personal social media, unless it is part of your work, such as a design Instagram.

Frequently asked questions

What should a college student put in their email signature?

Your full name, degree and major, university, expected graduation year and one way to contact you. Add LinkedIn or a portfolio when job hunting.

Should students put pronouns in their email signature?

It is optional and increasingly common at universities. If you include them, put them in brackets next to your name.

How should a PhD student sign emails?

Use “PhD Candidate” (after passing qualifying exams) or “PhD Student”, followed by your department and university, plus a link to your research profile.

Should I include my GPA in my email signature?

Usually not. Only consider it when applying for internships or jobs, and only if it is strong.

Do I need an email signature to email a professor?

It is not required, but it helps: your professor instantly sees your name, course and year without having to look you up.
