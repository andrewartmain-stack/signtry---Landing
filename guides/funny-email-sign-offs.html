Funny email sign-offs: 70+ unhinged, silly and work-safe ideas

Funny email sign-offs are the quickest way to make an email feel human. Here are more than 70 of them, from work-safe and witty to properly unhinged, plus a simple rule for when a joke works and when “Best regards” is still the right call.

Updated October 28, 2026 · 6 min read · By the SignTry team

Quick answer: A funny email sign-off works best with colleagues you already know, in casual threads, and when it matches the tone of the email. Keep it to one short line, and keep your normal signature with your name and contact details underneath it.

Funny email sign-offs that are safe for work

These get a smile without making HR nervous. Good for teammates, friendly clients and Friday emails.

  • Fueled by coffee and good intentions,
  • Sent from my desk, which misses me on weekends,
  • May your inbox be lighter than mine,
  • Running on caffeine and optimism,
  • Yours in spreadsheets,
  • Looking forward to your reply (and lunch),
  • Talk soon, unless this becomes a meeting,
  • With minimal typos,
  • Thanks in advance (and in arrears),
  • Cheers to fewer meetings,
  • Warmly, and slightly over-caffeinated,
  • Have a great weekend. You earned it,

Unhinged email sign-offs

For the group chat energy you bring to work. Use these only with people who already share your sense of humor.

  • Per my last email, and the one before that,
  • Sent from the void,
  • Holding it together with tape and hope,
  • This email will self-destruct in 5 business days,
  • Thriving (allegedly),
  • Respectfully, I need a nap,
  • Chaotically yours,
  • Living the dream (it is a weird dream),
  • Hope this email finds you before my next one does,
  • Signing off before I make it weird,
  • Sent with 14 browser tabs open,
  • Love and deadlines,

“Per my last email” is famously passive-aggressive. It is funny between friends, but it can sting with a client who genuinely missed something.

Gen Z email sign-offs

Short, lowercase and a bit ironic. Perfect for internal threads with a younger team; skip them for formal emails.

  • ok bye,
  • slay,
  • no thoughts, just vibes,
  • it is what it is,
  • lmk!,
  • ttyl,
  • live laugh leave me on read,
  • sending good energy,
  • this is a sign (off),
  • xoxo, gossip girl,

When to use a funny email sign-off

  • Yes: colleagues you know well, casual internal threads, creative industries, newsletters with a playful brand voice.
  • Maybe: long-term clients who joke with you first.
  • No: first contact, job applications, complaints, legal or financial topics, bad news, and anyone senior you have not met.

A good test: would you say the same line out loud to this person? If not, keep it professional.

Professional sign-offs for everything else

When in doubt, these never go wrong: Best regards, Kind regards, Best, Thanks, Thank you, Many thanks, Warm regards and Sincerely for formal letters. Put your sign-off on its own line, then your name, then your email signature.

Frequently asked questions

Is it professional to use a funny email sign-off?

It can be, with people you know well and in casual threads. For first contact, job applications, complaints or formal topics, use a standard sign-off such as “Best regards”.

What is an unhinged email sign-off?

A deliberately chaotic or over-honest closing line, such as “Thriving (allegedly)” or “Sent from the void”. They are popular on social media and best kept for close colleagues.

What is a good funny sign-off for work?

Something light and harmless, such as “Fueled by coffee and good intentions” or “May your inbox be lighter than mine”.

Should my sign-off go in my email signature?

Usually not. Type the sign-off yourself so you can match it to each email, and keep your signature for your name, title and contact details.
